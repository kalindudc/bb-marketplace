# Changelog

Notable changes to Tavern, newest first. Releases are tagged `tavern/vX.Y.Z` in this repository.

## Unreleased

Machine rules put a project's party members on their own new machines through the user's chosen providers, a returned or stopped quest reopens for deviations without a new quest, the checker recognises the check runners a repository actually uses, the pages and composer lose their rough edges, and the party is drawn as animated pixel art.

### Added

- The composer party reacts to reports and waypoint verdicts and says short, authored lines from its character files. Full voice adds occasional words and greetings; Light speaks after a verification, at a quest’s wrap or on a click. Off keeps the party quiet. Typing, asks and blockers silence the strip. Automatic speech also gives reports and evidence room to read, including the first minute after a report arrives; clicking can still speak. Each line shows in a small pixel bubble with a filled tail, beside the speaker and inside the strip.
- The composer draws its character on a single pixel-art canvas above the widget row, which keeps its class crest. Calm idles in place; Lively walks across the composer when the character is idle or working. Typing stops travel but keeps a quiet idle. An ask in the widget row holds the character still. Motion Off and reduced motion also show a still pose.
- Characters on the page share one animation scheduler. It pauses off screen and in hidden tabs and reduces physics updates if drawing and updating go over budget. The Characters setting still removes the strip without removing the class crest.
- The live character strip draws seeded avatars with rounded faces, varied hairstyles, and period clothing and tools chosen by class. The character file's avatar seed, or its id when no seed is set, keeps each look stable. The class tools and hats have distinct silhouettes. Verified characters give a small, warm smile and lift their tools; waiting, blocked and failed characters show a still pose.
- The character sheet groups bubble lines by mood and explains the tokens Tavern fills in. You can edit those lines in the voice editor and save them to your character override. The older voice slots are unchanged.
- The character sheet and its editor show the character's live avatar. Roll draws a new look, Put back returns to the saved one, and Keep saves the seed as `avatar` in your character override, so the composer and every other surface draw the same character. Traits pinned in the file's `look` stay fixed.
- The Keeper can name the repo for each hire with `repo`, a bb project id. Its `placement` pick is a rule id from `tavern_placements` that matches the repo and class, or where the Keeper runs for its own repo only. Consent, inputs, models and forge come only from the selected rule; no Keeper-supplied target inputs are merged. Discovery lists providers and their input schemas as information, earlier picks as rule ids, and the matching rule as the recommendation. A cross-repo hire starts in that project from its pushed default-branch tip, with none of the user's local unpushed commits or uncommitted files. A Warden defaults to its maker's repo. Without a checkout, or without a matching rule or own-repo pick, the hire refuses. Every hire's first line truthfully names its repo, rule or Keeper's pick, and target.
- Tavern records a placed member's first-turn model-resolution failure for its machine, environment and harness provider. A later hire with that same effective model refuses before allocation and names the class model to set in Where work runs. The failed member stops before archiving and releases without preserving an unstarted turn. Stop or release errors leave a visible release hold with Retry. Each rule card can clear the record. Temporary overload errors are not model-resolution records. There is no silent model substitution or fallback.
- The quest page offers Allow one more review when you want work to exceed the review budget. Only your explicit control can lift that hard limit; a Keeper cannot grant itself another review. Each allowance records its calling surface and is used for one hire.
- Stop all on the common room sends bb a stop for every known live Keeper and member, including members on other machines, without trusting a status read first. It holds new ticks, briefs, hires, spawns and pending cleanup until Resume, including across a restart. Anyone can pull it again while stopped; `bb tavern stop-all status` reads the stored receipt, with errors, confirmation times and the calling surface. Each thread has one five-second collection bound; an unconfirmed stop stays still stopping and updates when Tavern next observes its status. Quests, parties and machines stay as they are. Person-only messages pass, but a message queued together with Tavern\'s own work waits until you resume. Resume refuses plugin callers and any agent or CLI markers; bb does not distinguish an unmarked agent CLI call from the page, so its receipt says "Tavern can\'t prove a person pressed Resume" rather than attributing the action to you. Pause every Keeper sits beside Stop all and pauses new Keeper work while running turns finish.
- The quest hero has Hold and Resume buttons. Hold a quest from its page, or ask its Keeper to hold it in your own words. Every move line says when you held it and offers Resume. Held quests accept no new hires or briefs; running members finish their current step and keep their machines. Resume returns to the state before the hold. The Keeper can hold, resume or reopen in words only with your unused, uncancelled message routed to that quest after its last phase move; the receipt quotes your words. Open asks remain visible while held. News waits, then is delivered after Resume or Stop; pending first briefs wait for Resume. Pre-upgrade finished quests can be reopened using fresh routed words.
- While a quest is scouting, its move line says when the next news is due, using the earliest live Ranger time box or dated Keeper promise. With neither, it shows no promised time.
- A machine rule can choose a model, provider and reasoning level per class for members it places on other machines. Where work runs stages these choices behind Save rule. Hires verify the effective model and name the rule’s override in their first line; other hires keep their class settings.
- A Smith can open draft pull requests from home through a forge profile chosen by its machine rule. Where work runs lets you manage GitHub or command profiles. Tavern carries only the committed tree home, pushes the member’s own branch with a lease on later pushes, and shows the draft URL on the member card and hand-back. Existing rules keep pull requests on the member’s machine, where the Smith records a draft opened with its own tools. Each quest PR has a recorded state and owner; releasing its Smith passes open PR ownership to the Keeper without closing it.
- Machine rules place a Keeper's newly hired working members on their own new machines. An enabled rule in Settings, Where work runs, that matches the member's repo puts every new Ranger, Smith, Warden and Wanderer hired for that repo on its own new machine, through the environment and machine providers bb's catalog offers; Tavern has no provider of its own and depends on no other plugin. Keepers stay wherever the user started them and are never moved. A lead runs where its hiring Keeper runs, unless the Keeper picks a matching enabled rule with `placement`. Maker and checker slots per Keeper cap the machines in use, and waiting for a slot is a visible hold, never a quiet fallback to this machine. Only rules that ask for a new machine with a dedicated checkout run in this version; other targets save, preview, and block the hire with a plain reason.
- A placed member hired for the Keeper's own repo starts from the Keeper's exact source: the pushed base, the unpushed commits and, when the Keeper's checkout has uncommitted changes, its changed tracked and untracked files. Home reads its source through a temporary index. A placed member's snapshot reads committed changes and filesystem contents without writing Git objects or any index, then rebuilds and verifies the tree at home. The user's index, worktree, HEAD, refs, branches and stash are never touched; sparse checkouts are supported. The hire result's first line says where the member went, which rule placed it, and whether uncommitted changes came along and from where. The member's first turn calls `tavern_ready`, which binds its machine, carries the source in and delivers its scope. A placed member is labelled "Detection only (remote)"; when its scope changes, Tavern delivers the new scope to the machine itself and shows "Scope update pending" until the machine acknowledges it.
- A Warden checks a placed Smith's work on a machine of its own. Tavern waits for the Smith to finish its turn (it never interrupts it), brings the Smith's committed and uncommitted work home under a private ref, and carries that exact tree into the Warden's checkout. A verify or reject counts only while the Warden's checkout is clean and holds the submitted tree, and the maker's journal entries, submission, waypoint and assignment are unchanged; a newer submission supersedes the review. A placed Smith with a Warden whose rule gives it no separate machine blocks the hire and names the rule to add.
- Before Tavern lets a placed member's machine go (release, wrap, quest stop, archive, a finished errand, a cancelled hire), it saves the member's work and its declared file evidence home, then archives the thread so bb's normal cleanup removes the machine. A failed save keeps the thread and the machine and shows a blocker with Retry. Declared files that live outside the member's checkout and thread storage, or no longer exist, are listed as not saved with the reason. A member whose thread or machine was lost outside Tavern is released with a plain note that its work was not saved, never recorded as saved. The private snapshot refs Tavern keeps in the home checkout are described in `docs/machines-snapshots.md`.
- Every member surface (the ledger row, the quest page's member card, the widget and the member brief) shows where a placed member runs, the rule that placed it, its setup phase or blocker with Retry, and its posture. A machine that is still starting shows its provider's name, never an invented host name. The Keeper's ledger says where its party would run when a rule could apply to its project, and `tavern_status section=placement` gives the full picture per class with the slots in use.
- A returned or stopped quest can be re-opened for deviations and follow-up commits without opening a new quest. `tavern_reopen` returns it to scouting when no plan was greenlit, or to build otherwise, keeps its plan version and greenlight, clears its end and stop state, records `quest.reopened`, and reanchors the Keeper's thread. A live quest is refused; an archived quest must be brought up from the cellar first, and a deleted one restored from there.

### Changed

- A rule that places a Keeper must name keeper in its classes, or name no classes.
- Machine placement carries files with porcelain Git on members' and Wardens' machines without requiring member-side object or index plumbing. Changed-path queries and bounded tree/index listings provide full object IDs even when World Git abbreviates raw diffs. Successful commands may emit configuration warnings; their exit code determines success. Carries start only at the recorded commit with no foreign work, keep write-ahead marks for interruptions, and commit only the declared paths with Tavern's identity and the message `Tavern carried snapshot`, never impersonating the maker. Tavern never resets a member's branch. A changed HEAD blocks the carry and asks for preservation and reconciliation. Home computes each delta from the previous carried tree and verifies the rebuilt snapshot before a review or draft pull request can use it. Large-repository steps share a five-minute budget; quick Git reads retain their shorter limit.
- The recorded Agents may change settings grant still permits non-guardrail CLI changes when enabled. Managed threads cannot change guardrails, and the Settings-page RPCs remain user-only. Built-in classes follow the user’s party ceiling. Custom classes retain their smaller soft size, exceeded only by a one-use recorded decision and never past that ceiling. Review allowances are offered only at the budget and unused grants end with the quest. User receipts are passed explicitly into their own transactions, never inherited by background ticks, delivery or overdue promises.
- User-only controls share one authority policy. Plugin calls and client calls marked as an agent, thread or CLI are refused, including rule changes, Stop, Undo and route corrections. Unmarked clients remain unattributed: answer receipts say “answered from a client Tavern could not attribute”. The terminal refuses user-only commands from a thread.
- Asks carry their exact transition effect. An answer and its saved state changes commit together; delivery, scope installation and thread cleanup follow through their existing recovery paths. Old asks acquire a typed effect when read, and every withdrawal records its cause once. Class party size and Ranger capacity remain soft limits: a recorded reversible Keeper decision can exceed either once, but never the hard party ceiling or review budget.
- The Keeper's shorter class instructions leave more room for the live ledger. Its operating guide now covers judgment, briefs, checks, steers and recovery in short sections; tool descriptions carry their calling rules, and a reference document keeps the detailed guard and tick mechanics.
- Shell scopes use one catalogue of consequential acts, including pull request readiness, closing, merging and reviews, machine allocation, publishing, sends, deployments, credentials and configuration. A Smith may push, including with a lease and `--force-if-includes`, only to its recorded hire-assigned branch. PR edits and comments also require that head branch and the authenticated author. Checking out someone else’s PR never transfers ownership. The broad SC1 non-agent-command floor is reader-only. Smiths use an explicit authority catalogue rather than a five-command development whitelist, so plugin validation, query RPCs, research tools, workflow history, Git diff and gcloud auth login remain ordinary work. Gcloud deployments and secret access are deploy and credential acts. Machine reads, `gcloud config set` and secure `bb secrets request` calls are deliberate reader-floor exceptions for every class; local HTTP writes are not sends. PR fact preparation always checks redirects and invalidates later branch facts. Allowed acts have their own audit log, not a violation or attention warning. Hired Wanderers keep their previous open behavior until grants land. Keepers run recorded acts except Tavern’s user-only controls; Closing time refuses every catalogue act. Watch records without stopping, and scope messages no longer say “do not try another route”.
- Newly computed class scopes show every tool, including Tool Gateway discovery and backend enabling; existing members keep their saved lists until their scopes are regenerated. Members cannot start threads or workflows and are directed to their Keeper to hire; Keepers keep those capabilities. Local Pi scopes on macOS judge shell writes by effect through the sandbox, so interpreters, earlier path assignments, loops and heredocs no longer need a read-program exception. Rangers and Wardens can fetch a repository with a call-only Git metadata allowance; a Smith whose worktree is not yet bound works as a Ranger rather than stopping. Consequential shell acts remain caught and Watch still records them, but Watch does not contain or flag unresolvable writes. Linux and placed members retain their conservative shell judgment, while cortex reads and agent-facing writes and bb thread coordination are trusted on every platform. Thread archival stays Keeper-only. Tavern’s own user controls are refused in every scoped shell, including Keepers and Watch, regardless of option placement or whether the RPC handler has a client check. This includes rule changes, quest stops and decision or route corrections. Your scope mode does not change.
- Insights replaces the stored “In review” and “Wrapping” rows with “Held by you”, “Waiting to conclude”, “Stopping” and “Phases not recorded”.
- Older stops recorded only with `phaseBeforeStop` show their position as not recorded.
- Quest phases now move through one lifecycle, with the cause, actor and receipt recorded together. Review and wrap stages are read from current waypoint progress, not stored or dependent on proposing the plan again. The road, Insights and stop position use recorded transitions; older missing phase history is shown as not recorded, never inferred.
- The checker recognises the check runners a repository actually uses while keeping worktree confinement and the deny rules: any `npm`, `pnpm`, `yarn` or `bun run <script>`, `npx tsc` with or without `--noEmit`, a project binary reached directly (`tsc`, `vitest run`, `jest`), and a project binary reached through `node node_modules/…`. Other work (`npm install`, `npm publish`, `npx vitest` without `run`, `npx eslint`, scripts under `node scripts/…`, and path escapes) still needs exact-command metadata.
- The class page's records fold and remember what is open, the quest map's notes float over the map instead of reserving a band, and the rail keeps its calm order.
- The composer's ask row shows the whole question, a decision reads its note in place, and the composer no longer swallows the first click.
- A quest never concludes itself. The Keeper's final summary moves it to closing and opens an ask with three options: conclude the quest, keep it open for other work, or open extensions and side quests. The tale is told and the party archived only after your answer. Keeping it open or asking for extensions returns it to build; neither is the new held state. Retelling an already returned quest asks nothing new.
- A hire result names the resting party you could reuse, with thread ids, as advice rather than a refusal: re-brief a resting scout for a bounded follow-up, re-brief a resting builder only for the work it just finished, and never while a Warden is checking that work. A Warden is never re-briefed.
- A Keeper is woken by its own news, not only by the sweep. Filing a report, dispatching a party's hires, or answering an ask runs the same gated tick the sweep uses, so a routine report no longer waits out the interval. The Keeper's widget gains a new-event bubble counting what it has not seen, and a Batch wake events setting (off by default) may hold a wake briefly to gather a burst. Batching now belongs to the wake path alone: a regular sweep tick ignores the setting and always carries every unread event up to the packet limit.

### Fixed

- A Warden can now be hired for a machine-placed maker that has reported done. Tavern refused to capture a finished maker's submission, so no placed maker could ever be checked. A released or archived maker is still refused.
- Submission and preservation use Tavern's recorded source base, never the member's moving remote-tracking refs. Pushed-only preservation and draft imports stay in the private home repository, while forge commands read the user's checkout with optional Git locks disabled. PR records use the recorded fetch ref instead of an environment default. Uncommitted file/directory replacements and Git's executable-mode rules are preserved. Large carries batch their path queries and deletions, and cancelled deletion calls stop before changing more paths.
- Pushed-only source capture reads the remote's advertised default branch, not the local branch's upstream. It excludes local commits and dirty files. Missing objects are fetched only into Tavern's private home-import repository, never into the user's checkout.
- Machine placement checks that Git can carry the work before setup. Member endpoints probe porcelain only; home endpoints still require object and index plumbing. A machine missing the Git operations its role needs is a lasting blocker with Git’s reason, not a retry loop. Object formats are read from the verified commit and checked against repository configuration without requiring an optional Git query.
- Changing a class model no longer floods settings with repeated saves or leaves every class picker disabled. Each completed choice saves once after its menu closes, keeps the picker mounted and shows progress only on that row.
- Quests on the new-thread page use the same styled rows as the common room, with their Keeper, phase, path progress and whose move comes next. Quests with open asks or checker-handoff holds come first, newest first, and show how many things need you; greenlights count too, and a hold with a release ask counts once. The header includes holds in its count. Keeper labels stay off in the room and Ledger, and the homepage rows fit a phone without sideways scrolling.
- Long quest titles in the Ledger wrap to two lines with an ellipsis and a full-title tooltip. Heading counts wrap, and long gate names and row metadata no longer push past their cards.
- The Keeper's name keeps its width while the widget's quest titles shrink. Crowded quests become one Ledger-opening count chip at measured container steps: 576 px for one quest, 645 px for two, and 715 px for three. Only after collapse can the name ellipsize, and “need you” keeps its words down to 340 px.
- Insights fits narrow pages: long model ids wrap, chart columns can fit below 280 px, and fractional chart widths no longer scale the 11 px labels down.
- Startup adopts an open conclude ask on a building quest into closing with a recorded receipt. Stray conclude asks on finished quests are withdrawn as stale, never used to revive the quest. An invalid phase move leaves its answer unsaved and its ask open. Withdrawing a conclude ask returns closing to build, including archive and backup restore.
- Hovering a walker no longer paints a square background behind it. The strip has a transparent button that follows the actor and provides a visible focus outline for keyboard users.
- The walking strip sits where it should. Its CSS selector was a descendant rule that never matched the strip's own element (the element carries both classes), so the strip lost its height and position and the absolutely-placed sprite anchored to an ancestor and overlapped the composer and the ask row. The selector is corrected and the row plus strip share one banner root.
- A dark-mode character's eyes are dark again. The sprite's ink is a fixed dark in both themes, not bb's near-white dark foreground, so the eyes no longer turn white in dark mode.
- A member's own thread storage no longer opens a spurious hire-time scope ask. A `$BB_THREAD_STORAGE/**` or `${threadStorage}/**` write grant is already in effect, the same way temporary paths are, while star patterns, `..` and relative paths are still refused and a scope with no bound storage still asks.
- A Warden's checker model selection respects the configured class model: a model that is already a different family from the maker is kept, a replacement made to keep the check independent says so in the hire result, and a model the provider does not offer refuses before spawning with the class setting to change. The maker's own model is never counted as a different family, whatever the catalog calls it.
- A report's batch hold can no longer stack past one tick interval. The hold is capped at the Keeper's tick interval on the wake path, and every hold reason is logged, including the report batch that was previously silent. A report's reaction time from filing to delivery is now recorded.

## 0.1.2 (2026-11-15)

### Added

- A returned quest's tale shows the Keeper's full delivery as bb draws it in chat, interactive blocks from installed renderers included. Tavern adds a renderer that runs no model and draws the Keeper's final message in a hidden thread, with today's rendering as the fallback until it answers.
- Keepers can attach results to a tale, up to four figures and one chart, which Tavern draws on the tale page and on the tale card in the thread. A block the page cannot draw shows as a card that opens the Keeper's thread.
- Send a returned or stopped quest to the cellar, or delete it, from the quest page. Both archive the party's threads, and the Keeper's thread when it has nothing else on the board or out as a side quest. A deleted quest can be restored from the cellar's Deleted view at any time; credits stay on the character sheets. Bringing a quest back unarchives only its Keeper, and Keepers can bring archived members back with a new tool.
- The widget's Member panel shows who the member is and its scope as chips, an at-a-glance card, and the brief, context and follow-ups in remembered folds. The composer widget takes Tavern's warm surface with a character-colour edge.
- The sidebar's Common room link shows the hearth, lit, while quests are running.
- Tuned values can be edited by hand. Tavern keeps adapting from your value unless you pin it, and each value says what it does.

### Changed

- Keepers have full access: new and existing Keepers use the open scope preset, and Tavern keeps your trusted scope policy, `~/.agents/tavern/scopes.yml`, protected in every open scope. Members keep their presets and can use read-only renderer checks such as `check_ui`.
- Settings are grouped into fewer sections with cards that say what each group is for. Settings history is folded by default and each change reads in words.
- The map fills the page's height, and its notes float over the map instead of reserving a band.
- When nothing needs you, the common room shows one short line and moves What happened up.
- Every scope Tavern writes records the preset it came from. A pi session started before this release must restart to read the new field.

### Fixed

- The widget no longer fails to load for members whose Keeper sent long follow-ups.
- A stored event with an unrecognised actor no longer breaks the common room, Closing time's digest or backups; new events are checked when written.
- The member panel's guard words match what the member actually had, including for finished, released and archived members.

## 0.1.1 (2026-11-15)

### Added

- Every character's own name, with Roman numerals for repeats (Vex, Vex II), in thread titles, the composer widget, the map and the Keeper's ledger. Keepers can name party members in their tools, and names stay unique while a thread is live.
- A quest's tale leads with what the Keeper brought back: its final message, drawn with bb's own message renderer, then a "What to do now" list. A Keeper can retell the tale of a returned quest without repeating any of the wrap.
- One quest page for live, returned and stopped quests, with a wider rail whose sections fold and remember it, and a road through the five phases that shows where the quest is or where it stopped.
- A member's class and scope at a glance: the widget row reads "Pip · Wanderer", and the Member panel opens with who the member is and what its scope lets it do.
- The review queue expands each item to show its quest, waypoint, report and who it is waiting for.
- Archived Keepers' quests on the map, behind "Show archived Keepers".
- Motion settings with Calm, Lively and Off, including continuous ambient motion and its intervals.
- Agents can preview and apply Tavern settings from a thread, such as character models, reasoning, motion and voice.
- Every class may write to temporary folders.

### Changed

- Guardrail settings (scopes, machine rules, greenlight and decision timers, notifications and quiet hours, and the switch that lets agents change settings) apply only from a plain thread or the Settings page, never from a Keeper or party member.
- The member brief panel renders the brief as readable fields.

### Fixed

- A returned or stopped quest archives its whole party, including members released mid-quest. A one-time repair archives members stranded by earlier quests and leaves any thread you archived or unarchived yourself alone.
- Skipped waypoints are never counted as verified.
- The needs-you section of the composer widget stays collapsed when you switch threads.
- The Keeper's status and the CLI keep review items short, so long report outcomes no longer crowd out its quests and asks.

## 0.1.0 (2026-11-15)

The initial release.

### Added

- Delegate a goal through a Keeper, scouting, an approved quest map, builders and a separate checker, with asks and standing rules recorded in a ledger.
- Follow quests from the common room, connection map and quest pages, open reports in a side panel and read returned results with evidence and credits.
- Keep a journal in every thread, with steps, blockers, linked evidence, assigned verification and grouped Undo.
- Inspect classes, edit character presentation fields, configure providers and pacing, inspect Insights and save private backups from the page or CLI.
